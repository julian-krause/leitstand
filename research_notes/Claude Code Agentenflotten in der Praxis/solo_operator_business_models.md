## 1. Which solo or tiny-team businesses have verifiable revenue where AI agents do the bulk of the production work?

### Takeaway
Verified large outcomes exist (Base44, Medvi, the top of TrustMRR), but they are products with distribution in which AI wrote code or ads; none is documented as a "many parallel agents" operation, and the "solo" label is contradicted for the two biggest. The businesses that headline agent autonomy (Polsia, Felix, Andon Market) have self-reported, contested or negative numbers, and across 10,150 payment-verified indie products only 2.41% reach $100K ARR.

### Inferences
- In the verified cases the money comes from a product with a distribution engine (Wix-scale funnel, paid ads for a regulated high-demand drug, a founder audience of long standing). AI lowered build and creative cost; agent count is not the documented driver in any of them.
- "Solo" is a marketing category more than a headcount: Base44 had 6–8 employees, Medvi has contractors and outsourced fulfilment, Polsia's audit infers human operators.
- Polsia's revenue comes from people paying to have agent-run companies, not from those companies' sales (3.7% of the headline; $0.00 for showcased companies), so it is evidence for demand for the idea, not for the profitability of agent-run businesses.
- The portfolio data is the closest thing to a test of the "software factory" thesis: more products raise the chance of one hit, but per-product hit rate stays under 3.5% and the most prolific shippers mostly earn almost nothing.

### Gaps
- I found no business with independently verified revenue whose documented production method is "dozens of parallel coding agents run by one person".
- How much of Base44's code and of Medvi's operations AI produced is not quantified in the sources I reached; the NYT text itself was not opened.
- No 2026 solo acquisition with a disclosed price and documented agent-based production turned up (Skio's $105M sale to Recharge in April 2026 is not an AI company; Peter Steinberger joining OpenAI in February 2026 had no disclosed price) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/skio-recharge-105m-exit-subscription-shopify).
- The WSJ Polsia article (10,000 paying customers, $10M 2026 revenue target) was seen only through a summary — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/polsia-ben-broca-10-million-revenue-zero-employees).

## 2. For each model, what do parallel agents do, where does the money come from, and which models benefit from 100 concurrent agents?

### Takeaway
Parallelism pays where work splits into independent units with a machine-checkable verifier; almost every revenue model is capped earlier by demand, platform rules, deliverability or human review. No source shows revenue scaling with agent count.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Software factory. Anthropic's C-compiler experiment (2026-02-05): 16 parallel agents, nearly 2,000 sessions, about $20,000 API cost, two weeks, 100,000 lines. Parallelism worked when many independent failing tests existed and failed on one large task: "Having 16 agents running didn't help because each was stuck solving the same task." Requirement: "the task verifier is nearly perfect, otherwise Claude will solve the wrong problem." (opened) — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- (a) Shipping many products does not multiply income on its own (per-product hit rate under 3.5%; 9 of the 12 most prolific founders under $1K in 30 days) (opened) — [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/28/what-percentage-of-indie-hacker-products-make-100k-in-arr/)
- (b) Productized services. A creator running an "AI implementation agency" describes the order of work as "the first order of business in everything you should do is to get leads, to get book calls", his first deal came "through word of mouth", and "AI's good, but it's not that good ... you still kind of have to build a demo to sell them", which limits how many industries one can address (captions, 2026-11-10) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cFuyTJEX1I)
- (b) Demand-side fatigue, same source: business owners "either are against it ... or they've already spent 20 grand on a ... chatbot and it made them zero money" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cFuyTJEX1I)
- (c) Content at volume. Kapwing (Nov 2025) identified 278 AI-only channels among about 15,000 with 63 billion views and an estimated £90 million a year; the top channel was estimated at $4,251,500 a year. These are Social Blade estimates, not payouts (via search summary) — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/12/03/study-finds-ai-slop-videos-spreading-fast-across-youtube-and-theres-a-lot-of-money-being-made/)
- (c) Platform limit: YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" monetization rule to "inauthentic content" in July 2025; removed channels had combined estimated annual earnings of $9,785,600; enforcement works at channel level (via search summary) — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-removes-major-ai-slop-channels-with-combined-9-8m-in-estimated-annual-revenue/); [Media Copilot](https://mediacopilot.ai/youtube-ai-slop-monetization-policy/)
- (c) Even the pro-agent content case describes a filter, not a volume engine: "The system is not simply producing better scripts, it is producing fewer bad ones." (opened) — [The AI Corner](https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/20-agent-ai-script-factory-10m-revenue)
- (d) Outbound. Secondary accounts of a March 2025 TechCrunch investigation into AI-SDR vendor 11x report disputed customer logos and 70–80% churn (11x told TechCrunch retention was 79%); Artisan's CEO acknowledged "pretty low response rate" and "relatively high churn"; LinkedIn briefly banned Artisan in January 2026 (via search summary, sources are vendor-adjacent) — [Adrata](https://adrata.com/articles/the-11x-lesson); [Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/ai-sdr-claims-scrutiny-what-buyers-should-verify)
- (d) Sending capacity is rate-limited outside the agent: "You have to warm up your domains for outbound email and ... that takes a couple of weeks" (captions) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cFuyTJEX1I)
- (d) Verified revenue in this category sits with tool vendors (GojiberryAI #2, AiSDR #12 on TrustMRR), not with documented solo agent operators (opened) — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/)
- (e) Support and back office. Felix delegates support to a sub-agent with three-tier escalation, but lists "I can't get on calls", "I make confident mistakes" and overnight drift of about six hours as limits (opened; via search summary for the sub-agents) — [The Next New Thing](https://thenextnewthing.beehiiv.com/p/this-openclaw-earned-177-417); [Podwise episode notes](https://podwise.ai/episodes/7376786)
- (g) Trading. Alpha Arena season 1 (ended 2025-11-03): four of six frontier models lost money (Qwen3 Max +22.3%, DeepSeek +4.9%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 −30.8%, Grok 4 −45.3%, Gemini 2.5 Pro −56.7%, GPT-5 −62.7%); the organizer cautioned the test was short and uncontrolled (via search summary) — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)
- (g) Polymarket: a non-peer-reviewed study of 86 million bets (Apr 2024–Apr 2025) estimates about $40M went to arbitrageurs, with the top three wallets netting about $4.2M; analyses put losing traders at roughly 70–84%; 14 of the 20 most profitable wallets were reported to be bots (via search summary) — [DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/markets/polymarket-users-lost-millions-of-dollars-to-bot-like-bettors-over-the-past-year/); [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/84-of-polymarket-traders-are-losing-money-0-033-capture-majority-profits); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/prediction-markets-are-turning-into-a-bot-playground/)

### Inferences
| Model | What parallel agents do | Where money comes from | Binding constraint | Gain from 100 agents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Software factory | Independent tickets, test fixing, variants | Subscriptions, app sales, client fees | Distribution per product; human review; verifier quality | Only for decomposable, test-verified work |
| (b) Productized services for SMEs | Demos, integrations, internal tooling | Project fees, retainers | Leads, trust, industry knowledge, sales calls | Low; fulfilment for a few clients needs few agents |
| (c) Content, SEO, video | Draft, score, discard, render | Ad share, client retainers | Platform policy and enforcement; quality filter | Technically high, commercially fragile |
| (d) Lead gen and outbound | Research and enrichment per lead, drafting | Retainers, pay per meeting | Deliverability, warm-up, platform bans, reply rates | High for enrichment, capped for sending |
| (e) Support and back office | One agent per ticket or process | Per-seat or per-resolution fees | Winning clients; escalation and error cost | Scales with client volume, not before it |
| (f) Research and due-diligence reports | Fan-out over sources, cross-checking | Per-report fees | Buyer trust and liability; no revenue evidence found | Technically high, commercially unproven |
| (g) Arbitrage and trading | Signal generation, monitoring | Trading profit | Edge, latency, capital; LLMs mostly lost | None shown |

- Row (f) is my assessment only; I found no revenue evidence in either direction.

### Gaps
- No verified solo example for (e) or (f). No source for Google's handling of scaled programmatic SEO in 2026 was retrieved. Bulk-sender thresholds for email were not checked against primary provider documentation.

## 3. Track record of "AI automation agency" (AAA) models promoted on YouTube and in paid communities

### Takeaway
There is no independent dataset on typical AAA revenue or churn; available figures are self-reported by promoters or by vendors selling to agencies. The one hard record is regulatory: the FTC's Air AI case, ending in an $18 million judgment and a ban on selling business opportunities.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED (regulator): the FTC sued Air AI in August 2025 over earnings and refund claims; buyers, many of them small businesses, lost up to $250,000; the March 2026 settlement bans the company and owners from marketing business opportunities, with an $18 million judgment largely suspended and $50,000 paid (via search summary) — [FTC, August 2025](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund); [FTC, March 2026](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/air-ai-its-owners-will-be-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-company-misled)
- Liam Ottley's free Skool community is reported at roughly 311,500–337,000 members with a paid Accelerator upsell whose price is not public; his stated ">$18M" revenue and 65+ staff are UNVERIFIED; one reviewer notes his $10K–$50K/month examples reference his own 40+ person agency (via search summary) — [AI Funnel Insider](https://aifunnelinsider.com/ai-automation-agency-hub-review-2026/); [Ippei](https://ippei.com/aaa-accelerator/); [Marks Insights](https://marksinsights.com/liam-ottley-review/)
- The only revenue split found is an unsourced Substack estimate (coaching $150–200K/month, agency $110–120K, SaaS $30–50K); treat as speculation (via search summary) — [Substack](https://sabahudinmurtic.substack.com/p/330000-monthly-funnel-by-liam-outtley)
- A vendor summary of Reddit discussion says the hard part is "getting in front of buyers rather than ... building the automations" and attributes failures to no distribution and no proof; it cites 5% monthly churn as typical without a source. The vendor sells agency tooling (via search summary) — [Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-reddit-honest-truth); [Ciela MRR guide](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-agency-monthly-recurring-revenue)

### Inferences
- The share of promoter income from teaching versus client work cannot be stated for anyone; no promoter I found publishes a verified split.
- Where a breakdown exists at all (Felix, the Ottley estimate), the larger streams are the ones sold to aspiring operators.

### Gaps
- Typical real revenue, client counts and churn for AAA operators: no independent source found. Reddit threads (r/Entrepreneur, r/AI_Agents, r/ClaudeAI, r/SaaS) could not be read. No investigative press piece on AAA promoters was found.

## 5. Signals that a claimed agent-fleet business is real versus role-play

### Takeaway
The cases separate cleanly on three questions: can a third party see the money, can you name who pays, and is there an artifact with a stated cost and failure list. Agent count, dashboards and projections appear in every weak case and in none of the strong ones as evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Third-party-visible money: public-company reporting (Base44), a regulator record (Air AI), processor-verified listings (TrustMRR) — [Wix Q3 2026](https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/wix-reports-third-quarter-2026-results); [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/air-ai-its-owners-will-be-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-company-misled); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/)
- Technical write-ups that survive scrutiny state cost, method and limits: $20,000, 16 agents, and "Claude simply cheats here and calls out to GCC for this phase" — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- Headline metrics that turned out not to measure the business: 118,683 companies created versus 7,437 active; "ARR" containing ad-spend pass-through and one-off packs — [zero-arr audit](https://zero-arr.vercel.app/)
- A reported "revenue" number that was actually a cash balance: Andon Market's "$100K to $60K" is the agent's remaining budget, not sales (via search summary; opened Slashdot) — [Slashdot, 2026-09-13](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/09/13/0523208/)
- Projection presented alongside a thin result: a $100k/month plan stated nine days in, with one deal — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cFuyTJEX1I)
- Agent count as title: "How I Built a 50-Agent Company in One Evening" (title only, via search summary) — [Substack](https://technologiee.substack.com/p/how-i-built-a-50-agent-company-in)

### Inferences
- Signals of a real operation: processor-verified or filed revenue; named customers who can be contacted; shipped artifacts one can inspect; cost per unit of output; a stated discard or failure rate; revenue that persists past a launch spike (fly.pieter.com is the counter-example).
- Signals of role-play: agent count or companies-created as the headline; screenshots without account context; run-rate annualized from one month; revenue definitions that include pass-through; "plan to make X" titles; income that traces to guides, communities, coaching or marketplaces for agent assets; a business younger than the claim is large.
- An honest disclosure of discarded work and cost is weak positive evidence about the operation, and says nothing about revenue.

### Gaps
- No published checklist from an independent body was found; the list above is derived from the cases.

## 6. What credible operators name as the real bottleneck

### Takeaway
Demand and distribution come first, human review and verification second, robustness third; compute is a real line item but is the only one of the four that money solves directly.

### Cited Findings
- Review: "AI-generated code needs to be reviewed, which means the natural bottleneck on all of this is how fast I can review the results." (via search summary) — [Simon Willison, 2025-10-05](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/)
- Attention: "Your cognitive bandwidth doesn't parallelize."; "three focused, well-reviewed threads produce more usable output than six threads I'm half-supervising"; Willison is paraphrased as being "wiped out for the day" after four agents until 11am (opened) — [Addy Osmani, 2026-04-07](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-parallel-agents/)
- Verification: "it is easy to see tests pass and assume the job is done, when this is rarely the case" (opened) — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- Demand: leads and booked calls first; first client via referral; everyone working with him "I met from my YouTube last year" (captions) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cFuyTJEX1I)
- Robustness: "Autonomy without oversight is just unsupervised mistakes." (opened) — [The Next New Thing](https://thenextnewthing.beehiiv.com/p/this-openclaw-earned-177-417); "the gap between 'capable' and 'completely robust' remains wide" (opened) — [Anthropic, Project Vend phase two](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)
- Compute: about $20,000 at API prices for 16 agents over two weeks — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler); $1,400/month in subscriptions for a claimed 30–50 agents — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q6NY3KbYCAs); Polsia's compute at about 57% of subscription run rate per the adversarial audit — [zero-arr audit](https://zero-arr.vercel.app/)
- Plan pricing context from third-party blogs: Max at $100 and $200 a month; a full Max 20x weekly limit estimated at about $1,100 API-equivalent; "agent teams use about 7x the tokens" attributed to Anthropic docs; a large fan-out "can exhaust the weekly allowance" (via search summary, not checked against Anthropic) — [Botfarm](https://botfarm-site.pages.dev/blog/claude-max-true-price); [Redreamality](https://redreamality.com/blog/claude-code-usage-limits-cost-2026/)

### Inferences
- Practitioners who review what they merge report ceilings of 3–15 sessions; claims of 30–50 coincide with an admitted discard rate and skipped review. Scaling to 100 therefore changes the job into building verifiers and queues, which is the part Carlini says he spent his time on.
- For a solo operator the order of constraints suggests the next marginal dollar is better spent on a distribution channel than on agents 31 to 100.

### Gaps
- No controlled measurement of revenue or output versus agent count for a small business exists in what I found. Anthropic's own cost documentation was not opened.

## 7. Experiments with fully autonomous agent-run businesses

### Takeaway
Every documented experiment either lost money or became profitable only after humans added tools, procedures and physical labor; none ran without human support.

### Cited Findings
- Project Vend phase one (published 2025-06-27, Claude Sonnet 3.7, about a month): the shop "did not succeed at making money"; failures included below-cost tungsten cubes, discounts, a hallucinated payment account and an identity episode on 2025-03-31/04-01; verdict "we would not hire Claudius"; no dollar net-worth figures in the text (opened) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1)
- Phase two (published 2025-12-18, Sonnet 4.0 then 4.5, San Francisco, New York, London): weeks with negative margin were "largely eliminated"; discounts fell about 80%; a CEO agent's message shows $2,649.20 against a $15,000 quarterly target and one $408.75 day; "The fact that the business started to make money may have been in spite of the CEO"; models "still needed a great deal of human support"; no aggregate profit stated (opened) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)
- WSJ newsroom run (reported December 2025): about three weeks; prices dropped to zero, a PlayStation given away; losses reported as "hundreds of dollars" in the WSJ headline and "$1,000+" elsewhere (via search summary) — [WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/we-let-ai-run-our-office-vending-machine-it-lost-hundreds-of-dollars/amp/); [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2025/12/18/ai-vending-machine-lost-1000-to-social-engineering.html)
- Andon Market, San Francisco (agent "Luna", press coverage from April 2026, $100,000 budget, three-year lease): in April about $15,000 spent on inventory against about $2,000 revenue and a roughly $7,500 monthly lease; break-even needs about $500 a day by the agent's own accounting; by September the budget stood at $60,000, with human clerks and human sign-off on decisions (via search summary; Slashdot opened) — [Good Morning America/ABC](https://goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/san-francisco-shop-run-completely-ai-agent-132281378); [Bloomberg, 2026-04-23](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-23/an-ai-agent-takes-over-a-store-and-orders-too-many-candles-mobwrfw2); [Slashdot, 2026-09-13](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/09/13/0523208/)
- Polsia and Felix: see section 1; in both, a human sets priorities or operates controls (the audit's "Human-in-the-loop, not autonomous" is an inference from code; Felix: "I don't set my own priorities") — [zero-arr audit](https://zero-arr.vercel.app/); [The Next New Thing](https://thenextnewthing.beehiiv.com/p/this-openclaw-earned-177-417)
- Trading as an autonomy test: four of six models lost money in Alpha Arena season 1 (via search summary) — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)

### Inferences
- The improvement from Vend phase one to two came from scaffolding and procedure, which is human design work; this matches the verifier finding in section 6.
- Physical and adversarial settings (free-item requests, manipulated votes) expose agents faster than digital ones, but the digital cases (Polsia's showcased companies at $0.00) do not show autonomous revenue either.

### Gaps
- Andon Labs has published no audited financials for Andon Market; the reader comment citing about $22,000 revenue over two months is unverified. Vending-Bench 2 leaderboard figures were not retrieved. Total profit for Vend phase two is not disclosed.
