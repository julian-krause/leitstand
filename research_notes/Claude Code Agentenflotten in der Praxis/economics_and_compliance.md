# Unit economics and legal/platform constraints of a ~100-agent fleet run by a solo operator in Germany (state: 14 November 2026)

Research date: 14 Nov 2026. All prices in USD unless marked EUR. Orientation only, not legal or tax advice.
Source tags used below: **[P]** = primary/official page opened and read on 14 Nov 2026; **[S]** = secondary source opened and read; **[SR]** = taken from a search-result summary only, page not opened (verify before quoting). All arithmetic that is mine is placed under "Inferences" and labelled.

## 1. Token burn in practice: tokens and dollars per agent-hour and per completed task

### Takeaway
Sourced data points converge on roughly **$3–9 of list-price API cost per active agent-hour on Opus-class models** (about $2.60 on Sonnet 5.5, about $0.2–0.9 on Haiku 5.5 for the same token profile) and **about $5–10 per completed ticket-sized coding task**; multi-agent orchestration multiplies this (Anthropic: agent teams use about 7x the tokens of a single session). Published "swarm" projects cost $1.3k–$20k per project, and the cost is dominated by how many tokens the workers burn, not by the planner.

### Cited Findings
- **Official Claude Code average [P]:** "Across enterprise deployments, the average cost is around $13 per developer per active day and $150-250 per developer per month, with costs remaining below $30 per active day for 90% of users." This is interactive human-driven use, not an autonomous fleet. — [Claude Code docs: Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)
- **Official multiplier for multi-agent work [P]:** "Agent teams use approximately 7x more tokens than standard sessions when teammates run in plan mode, because each teammate maintains its own context window"; "token usage is roughly proportional to team size." — [Claude Code docs: Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)
- **Official example of a session's token mix [P]:** the docs' sample `/usage` output shows `Total cost: $0.55`, `Total duration (API): 6m 20s`, `claude-sonnet-4-6: 1.2k input, 5.3k output, 940.0k cache read, 50.0k cache write`. This is an illustrative sample, not a measurement, but it shows the typical shape: about 94% of tokens are cache reads. — [Claude Code docs: Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)
- **Anthropic parallel-agent experiment (5 Feb 2026) [P]:** 16 parallel agents, Opus 4.6, "across two weeks", "nearly 2,000 Claude Code sessions", "2 billion input tokens", "140 million output tokens", "a total cost just under $20,000", 100,000-line Rust C compiler. Anthropic's own comment: "Compared to even the most expensive Claude Max plans, this was an extremely expensive project." — [Anthropic Engineering: Building a C compiler](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler); press coverage [The Register, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/09/claude_opus_46_compiler/)
- **Gas Town, measured by DoltHub (16 Apr 2026) [S]:** Neil Macneale: the work "took about two weeks and cost about $6,000 in Claude API calls"; peak of 13 polecats running at once plus Mayor and support agents; "There was a day where agents were dying frequently because we ran out of tokens"; "I strongly suspect this would be cheaper with a Max subscription, which I'm testing now." No token counts or model named. — [DoltHub: Two Weeks in Gas Town](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-16-two-weeks-in-gastown); earlier post used "DoltHub's metered Anthropic subscription", not Max — [DoltHub: A Week in Gas Town, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-03-24-a-week-in-gas-town) [SR]
- **Community measurement, autonomous ticket pipeline (20 Mar 2026) [S]:** Botfarm author ran an autonomous Claude Code pipeline (about 95% Opus 4.6 at $5/$25) and logged API-equivalent cost per task: Max 5x window (21 h): 32 of 33 tasks completed, 189 pipeline stage runs, $152 API-equivalent; Max 20x window (46 h): 42 of 47 tasks, 308 stage runs, $253 API-equivalent. Author's caveats: lower bounds, opaque utilization metric, two weekly cycles and 80 tasks only. — [Botfarm: The True Price of a Claude Max Weekly Limit](https://botfarm-site.pages.dev/blog/claude-max-true-price)
- **Cursor swarm economics (20 Jul 2026) [P]:** rebuild SQLite in Rust from the 835-page manual; total run cost ranged "from $1,339 for the Opus 4.8 hybrid [Opus 4.8 planner + Composer 2.5 workers] to $10,565 for GPT-5.5 alone"; worker-only cost $411 vs $9,373; workers were at least 69% of tokens in every run and over 90% in most; in the hybrid the planner was a small token share but roughly two-thirds of cost; every mix "produced similar quality, but the costs varied enormously." — [Cursor: Agent swarms and the new model economics](https://cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics); secondary analysis [Digital Applied, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/cursor-agent-swarm-sqlite-rust-planner-worker-economics)
- **Cursor browser experiment (Jan 2026) [SR]:** agents ran close to a week, over 1 million lines of code across 1,000 files; Cursor did not publish a total cost; a critic's article claims 10–20 trillion tokens and multi-million-dollar cost and disputes the quality (repo "barely compiles"). Treat the dollar figure as unverified. — [Cursor: Scaling agents](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents); [Fortune, 23 Jan 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/01/23/cursor-built-web-browser-with-swarm-ai-agents-powered-openai/); critic: [Let's Data Science](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/cursor-misrepresents-ai-built-web-browser-project-f61f9408)
- **Stripe / Ramp background agents [SR]:** Stripe Minions: over 1,300 agent-generated PRs merged per week as of Feb 2026 (human-reviewed, no human-written code); Ramp Inspect: over half (one source: 57%) of merged PRs. Neither company has published cost per PR. — [Stripe: Minions](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents); [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-stripes-minions-ship-1300-prs); [SoftwareSeni on Ramp/Stripe measurement](https://www.softwareseni.com/how-ramp-dropbox-and-stripe-measure-the-impact-of-ai-coding-agents/)
- **A cautionary solo-operator data point [S]:** a self-reported 30-day run of an always-on agent setup on a $200 plan burned 71% of the allocation in days 1–3, found a 5-agent team used "27% daily budget in 45 min" for the same output a solo agent produced with about 8%, and earned $4.99 revenue against $200 cost. Self-reported, author sells related tools. — [DEV: The Token-Per-Dollar Math](https://dev.to/yurukusa/the-token-per-dollar-math-running-claude-max-for-30-days-2k1o)

### Inferences
- **Token profile per active hour (my scaling of the official docs sample):** 6m20s of API time scaled to 60 min gives about 8.9M cache-read tokens, 0.47M cache-write tokens, 0.05M output tokens, 0.011M uncached input tokens, about 9.4M tokens per API-hour. At Sonnet 4.6 prices this is $5.24 per API-hour ($0.55 x 9.47). I use this as "Profile A" for all price comparisons below. It likely understates output/thinking tokens for high-effort autonomous work.
- **C compiler, cost per agent-hour (my arithmetic):** $20,000 / (16 agents x 14 days x 24 h = 5,376 agent-hours) = at least $3.72 per agent-hour if all agents ran continuously; higher per active hour if they did not. About $10 per session ($20,000 / ~2,000 sessions). Blended about $9.35 per million tokens ($20,000 / 2.14B).
- **Gas Town (my arithmetic):** $6,000 / 14 days is about $430 per calendar day for a fleet peaking at about 13–15 agents. If the fleet effectively ran 8 h on 10 working days, that is about $75 per fleet-hour or about $5 per agent-hour at peak concurrency. The assumption on hours is mine, not DoltHub's.
- **Per completed task (my arithmetic from Botfarm):** $152 / 32 = $4.75 and $253 / 42 = $6.02 API-equivalent per completed ticket-sized task on Opus 4.6; about $0.80 per pipeline stage run.
- **Range to plan with:** $3–9 per active Opus-class agent-hour at list API prices; $5–10 per completed ticket-level task; a 7x multiplier when moving from single sessions to agent teams. The Cursor result implies the cheapest lever is the worker model, since workers are 69–90%+ of tokens.

### Gaps
- No primary Yegge figure for Gas Town's daily cost was found. A "$100 per 60-minute session" figure and a "$10k+/month per developer" figure appeared only in search-result summaries of secondary write-ups and could not be traced to a primary post.
- No ccusage dataset with hours-of-runtime attached was found; public ccusage screenshots report dollars and tokens but not agent-hours, so $/agent-hour had to be derived.
- No cost-per-PR numbers exist publicly for Stripe Minions or Ramp Inspect.
- Almost all measured data is for coding agents. I found no measured token burn for non-coding knowledge-work agents (research, outreach drafting, document processing).
- Whether Anthropic's "2 billion input tokens" includes cache reads is not stated, so tokens-per-agent-hour from that project (at least ~0.4M/h) is not comparable to Profile A (~9.4M/h).

## 2. Subscription versus API: what one Max/Ultra plan sustains, how many plans 100 agents would need, and ban/limit reports

### Takeaway
As of the official pricing page on 14 Nov 2026 the $200 plan is called **Ultra** and gives "10x Pro usage every week" with no 5-hour limit for verified accounts (Max at $100 gives 5x). The only careful community measurement (Feb–Mar 2026, Opus 4.6) put the $200 plan's weekly cap at about **$1,100 of API-equivalent usage per week (about $4,730/month)**, a roughly 20x subsidy versus API list prices, which is exactly why Anthropic has tightened automated and third-party use three times in 2026. A 100-agent fleet at 20% duty cycle would need on the order of 7–9 such plans on paper, but consumer plans carry opaque caps, no data processing agreement, and a documented enforcement history.

### Cited Findings
- **Current plans and prices [P, 14 Nov 2026]:** Pro $20/mo ($17 annual); Max $100/mo, "5x Pro usage every 5 hours and every week"; Ultra $200/mo, "No 5-hour limit" (verified accounts) and "10x Pro usage every week"; Fable included only "up to half of weekly limit" on Max and "50% of weekly limits" on Ultra; Max includes $100/month and Ultra $200/month in API credits for the Claude Platform; Team Premium seat $100/mo annual ($125 monthly), 5x standard seats; Enterprise $20/seat/month (20-seat minimum) "plus usage at API rates". — [claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)
- **Limits mechanics [P]:** Pro and Max have a five-hour session limit and a weekly limit; Ultra has a weekly limit and verified Ultra accounts have no five-hour limit; Max and Ultra have a separate weekly limit for Fable; limits are shared across Claude and Claude Code; usage credits beyond the limit are billed at standard API rates; "Monthly API credits included with Max and Ultra cannot be used in Claude Code or the Claude apps"; if `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set, Claude Code bills the API instead of the plan. — [Claude Help Center: Using Claude Code with your Pro or Max plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-using-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan); Anthropic reserves the right to "limit your usage in other ways, such as weekly and monthly caps or model and feature usage, at our discretion" — [Claude Help Center: What is the Max plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11049741-what-is-the-max-plan)
- **Rename [SR]:** a third-party blog reports the $200 plan stopped being called Max 20x on 30 Oct 2026 and became Ultra, with "weekly limits remain unchanged". The official pricing page confirms the Ultra name and the 10x weekly multiple but not the date. — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/claude-max-plan-changes-ultra-5-hour-limit-2026); [Let's Data Science: five-hour limit removed for verified Ultra](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-removes-five-hour-claude-limit-for-verified-ultra-56c3c5dc)
- **Measured weekly budget (20 Mar 2026) [S]:** Max 5x: 28% of weekly utilization consumed by $152 of API-equivalent work, so about $5.44 per 1%, about $523/week, about $2,250/month. Max 20x: 23% consumed by $253, so about $10.99 per 1%, about **$1,100/week, about $4,730/month**. The $200 plan gave "roughly 2x" the weekly budget of the $100 plan (not 4x). Workload about 95% Opus 4.6. Author notes Anthropic has since throttled the usage-API polling used for the measurement. — [Botfarm](https://botfarm-site.pages.dev/blog/claude-max-true-price); companion post with utilization data (3,664 snapshots, 22 Feb–1 Mar 2026; the $200 plan's weekly budget about 2.1x the $100 plan's) — [DEV: Claude Max $100 vs $200, measured](https://dev.to/alexdobrushskiy/claude-max-100-vs-200-what-you-actually-get-measured-not-guessed-3kjd)
- **Last official hour estimates are from 2025 [SR]:** when weekly limits were introduced (announced 28 Jul 2025), Anthropic estimated Max 20x at roughly 240–480 hours of Sonnet and 24–40 hours of Opus per week, varying with codebase size; Anthropic said the limits were aimed at a small number of users who share accounts and resell access to Claude Code. Guides in 2026 say these hour figures are stale and no current official figure exists. — [TechCrunch, 28 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/28/anthropic-unveils-new-rate-limits-to-curb-claude-code-power-users/); [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/anthropic-claude-code-weekly-limits-september-14/)
- **Enforcement timeline against third-party/automated subscription use [SR, multiple outlets agree]:** 9 Jan 2026: Anthropic began blocking subscription OAuth tokens in third-party clients; 19 Feb 2026: documentation update stating that OAuth tokens from Free/Pro/Max plans may not be used in third-party tools; some accounts were "automatically banned for triggering abuse filters" and Anthropic said it reversed those; 4 Apr 2026: third-party harnesses such as OpenClaw no longer draw on subscription limits and are billed as extra usage, with Anthropic citing unsustainable compute costs. — [Gigazine, 20 Feb 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260220-anthropic-third-party-block); [WinBuzzer, 19 Feb 2026](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/); [Implicator on OpenClaw](https://www.implicator.ai/anthropic-cuts-openclaw-from-claude-subscriptions-citing-unsustainable-compute-costs/); [PiunikaWeb, 19 Feb 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-claude-max-ban-agent-sdk-clarification/)
- **Multi-account bans [SR, disputed]:** in mid-Feb 2026 users with several Max subscriptions reported bans; Anthropic's Thariq Shihipar is quoted: "We haven't changed anything here. It's not against terms of service to have multiple MAX accounts", with enforcement aimed at reselling. The quote is reported secondhand; a user-reported support answer says one person may hold multiple accounts but sharing one account among several people violates the terms. — [MetricNexus](https://metricnexus.ai/blog/anthropic-banning-multiple-claude-accounts)
- **Programmatic use on subscriptions [SR]:** on 14 May 2026 Anthropic announced that `claude -p` and Agent SDK usage would move to a separate monthly credit from 15 Jun 2026 ($20 Pro, $100 Max 5x, $200 Max 20x); several outlets then reported Anthropic paused the change. — [The Register, 14 May 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-ml/2026/05/14/anthropic-tosses-agents-into-the-api-billing-pool/5240748); [Let's Data Science: pause](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-pauses-claude-agent-sdk-billing-overhaul-1cff2071)
- **API discounts [P]:** cache reads cost 0.05x of base input on Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 ($0.20 and $0.10 per MTok) and 0.025x on Fable 5.2 ($0.20); 5-minute cache writes 1.25x, 1-hour writes 2x; Batch API "50 percent discount on both input and output tokens", stackable with caching; Batch is not available for Managed Agents sessions or fast mode. — [Claude API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- **OpenAI plans [SR, sources conflict]:** ChatGPT Pro $100 tier launched Apr 2026 with 5x Plus's Codex usage; $200 tier 20x; Pro plans reportedly have no five-hour window but weekly limits apply; in Oct 2026 OpenAI reportedly doubled limits for "Pro 100/200/500 tiers and Business Premium"; one source says new $200 sign-ups were paused since 10 Sep 2026. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/openai-introduces-chatgpt-pro-usd100-tier-with-5x-usage-limits-for-codex); [The Next Web, 12 Apr 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/openais-new-100-chatgpt-pro-plan-targets-claude-max-with-five-times-the-codex-access); [BuildMVPFast on the doubling](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/chatgpt-pro-limits-doubled-vs-claude-max-2026); [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/codex-usage-limits-pricing-2026)
- **Google plans [SR, sources conflict]:** Jules limits reported as Free 15 tasks/day (3 concurrent), Pro 100/day (15 concurrent), Ultra 300/day (60 concurrent); AI Ultra reported at $99.99 (5x Pro) or $199.99 (20x) as of Oct 2026, older coverage says $249.99; Google says it "may adjust limits and features". — [Developers Digest: Jules](https://www.developersdigest.tech/coding-agent-plans/jules); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/google-ai-pro-vs-ultra)

### Inferences
- **How long one $200 plan runs an Opus agent (my arithmetic from Botfarm):** 23% of the weekly cap was consumed in a 46-hour window, so the full weekly cap corresponds to about 200 hours of that pipeline, i.e. roughly one always-on Opus pipeline per $200 plan (Feb–Mar 2026 conditions). The $100 plan: 28% in 21 h, about 75 hours per week, under half an always-on agent. Parallelism inside the Botfarm pipeline is not documented, so this is rough.
- **Subsidy factor:** $4,730 of API-equivalent usage for $200 is about 24x; per task, 23% of a $50/week plan is $11.50 for 42 tasks, about $0.27 per task versus about $6.02 at API prices.
- **Plans needed for 100 agents at 20% duty, 8 h/day (3,520 active agent-hours/month on 22 days):** at the Opus 4.6 Profile A rate of $8.73/h this is about $30,700 of API-equivalent usage per month, i.e. 6.5 plan-months at $4,730, so 7 plans ($1,400/month); on 30 days 9 plans ($1,800/month). This assumes the Mar 2026 weekly cap still holds, that all usage is one model family, and ignores the Fable 50% sub-cap.
- **Plans needed for 100 agents truly concurrent 24/7 (16,800 agent-hours/week):** 85 plans by the 200-hours-per-plan estimate, about 135 plans by the dollar estimate, i.e. $17k–27k/month, versus about $374k/month at Opus 5.5 API list price (72,000 h x $5.20). Nobody should expect Anthropic to tolerate that pattern on consumer plans; the weekly limits were introduced in 2025 against heavy outlier use, account sharing and reselling, and Anthropic's own C-compiler write-up calls a $20k project "extremely expensive" relative to Max plans.
- **Practical reading:** the subscription route is 10–20x cheaper on paper but (a) the cap is opaque and has been changed several times in 12 months, (b) the Agent SDK / `claude -p` billing status has been announced, paused and may change again, (c) consumer plans are not covered by the business data processing addendum (see section 5), so they are unsuitable for client data regardless of price.

### Gaps
- No measurement of the Ultra weekly cap after the Oct 2026 rename, and none on current models (Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 with $0.20 cache reads). The $1,100/week figure may be off by a large factor in either direction.
- Current, authoritative status of Agent SDK / headless usage on Pro/Max/Ultra could not be confirmed from an Anthropic page; the two Help Center articles opened do not address it.
- An unopened article title ("Same $200 Claude Plan, Two Limits: One Gets 20% Less a Week", FourWeekMBA) suggests cohort differences in weekly caps; not verified.
- No primary source for the multi-account statement; no reliable count of banned accounts (an "11.4M bans in H1 2026" claim comes from a low-quality page).
- OpenAI and Google plan limits are from conflicting secondary sources; I found no user measurement of their API-equivalent weekly value.

## 3. Cheaper routing: small and open-weight model prices, and the reported quality penalty

### Takeaway
For the same token profile, Haiku-class and Chinese flash-class models are roughly **6x to 40x cheaper per active agent-hour** than Opus 5.5 ($0.13–0.87 versus $5.20), and Cursor's July 2026 experiment shows a frontier planner with cheap workers reaching the same end quality at about one-eighth the total cost. The reported penalty is on long-horizon, loosely specified work and on speed, so cost per finished task (including retries and review) is the number to track, not price per token.

### Cited Findings
- **Anthropic list prices per MTok [P, 14 Nov 2026] (input / output / 5m cache write / cache read):** Fable 5.2 $8 / $40 / $10 / $0.20; Opus 5.5 $4 / $20 / $5 / $0.20; Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10 / $2.50 / $0.10; Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50 / $0.125 / $0.01 for prompts up to 100,000 tokens and $0.50 / $2.50 / $0.625 / $0.05 above; Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5 / $1.25 / $0.10. Batch halves input and output. US-only inference 1.1x; Bedrock/Vertex regional endpoints +10%. — [Claude API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- **OpenAI list prices per MTok [P, 14 Nov 2026] (input / cached input / output):** gpt-6-astra $10 / $1 / $50; gpt-5.3-codex $1.75 / $0.175 / $14; gpt-5.4-mini $0.75 / $0.075 / $4.50; gpt-5.4-nano $0.20 / $0.02 / $1.25; Batch and Flex about half; regional (data residency) endpoints +10% for models released on or after 5 Mar 2026. — [OpenAI API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)
- **Google list prices per MTok [P, 14 Nov 2026]:** Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview $2 input / $12 output / $0.20 cached (prompts up to 200k; $4 / $18 / $0.40 above); Gemini 3.8 Flash $0.75 / $3.75 / $0.075 through 31 Dec 2026, **doubling to $1.50 / $7.50 / $0.15 from 1 Jan 2027**; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite $0.30 / $2.50 / $0.03; batch half price; explicit cache storage billed per hour. — [Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)
- **DeepSeek list prices per MTok [P, 14 Nov 2026] (cache hit / cache miss / output):** deepseek-flash (V4.1 Flash) peak $0.006 / $0.30 / $1.20, off-peak half; deepseek-v4-pro peak $0.044 / $1.32 / $3.96, off-peak half; peak hours 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC Mon–Fri. — [DeepSeek API pricing](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)
- **Kimi / GLM / Qwen [SR, mid-2026 blogs that disagree]:** Kimi K3.1 Pro reportedly $2 input / $10 output (launched 23 Oct 2026); GLM-5 $1.00 / $3.20 (Sep 2026 listing); Qwen3.7 Plus output about $1.28; coding subscriptions Kimi Code $15/mo, GLM Coding Plan Lite $18/mo, Qwen Code Pro $50/mo. Verify on vendor pages. — [devtk.ai](https://devtk.ai/en/blog/kimi-k3-vs-glm-5-2-deepseek-v4-2026/); [Capital & Compute](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/china-ai-pricing/); [Apidog](https://apidog.com/blog/chinese-llm-price-war-2026/)
- **Routing evidence [P]:** Cursor: Opus 4.8 planner + Composer 2.5 workers $1,339 total versus $10,565 for GPT-5.5 alone, all configurations eventually reaching 100% on the held-out suite; "less expensive models simply have to follow it [the plan]", "a huge potential source of cost savings"; with a Fable 5 planner the run cost substantially more because workers used several times as many tokens. — [Cursor: Agent swarms and the new model economics](https://cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics)
- **Anthropic's own guidance [P]:** "Use Sonnet for teammates"; "For simple subagent tasks, specify `model: haiku`". — [Claude Code docs: Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)
- **Quality penalty as reported [SR]:** open-weight models (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM) are within about 1–2 points of closed leaders on SWE-bench-Verified-style tasks, but the gap widens on private and long-horizon benchmarks (one estimate: 4–6 months behind on public, 8–10 months on private tests); a 410-task comparison found Kimi cost about the same per solved task as Sonnet but was much slower (median hard-tier run 98 s versus 10–20 s); practitioners advise measuring cost per finished task on your own work; concern that open models train closer to public tests. — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/open-weight-models-vs-frontier-ai-coding); [Latitude](https://latitude.so/blog/i-added-an-open-weights-model-to-my-coding-benchmark); [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ai-model-for-coding); [Kimchi](https://kimchi.dev/blog/glm-5-2-vs-frontier-what-the-data-says)

### Inferences
- **Profile A priced per active agent-hour at Nov 2026 list prices (my arithmetic; 8.905M cache read, 0.474M cache write or uncached input, 0.050M output, 0.011M input; no cache-write surcharge assumed for OpenAI/Google/DeepSeek):**

  | Model | $/active agent-hour |
  |---|---|
  | gpt-6-astra | 16.27 |
  | Claude Fable 5.2 | 8.62 |
  | Claude Opus 5.5 | 5.20 |
  | Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview | 3.35 (excl. cache storage) |
  | gpt-5.3-codex | 3.11 |
  | Claude Sonnet 5.5 | 2.60 |
  | gpt-5.4-mini | 1.26 |
  | DeepSeek V4 Pro | 1.23 peak / 0.62 off-peak |
  | Gemini 3.8 Flash | 1.22 until 31 Dec 2026, 2.44 from 1 Jan 2027 |
  | Claude Haiku 5.5 | 0.17 (prompts up to 100k) / 0.87 (prompts over 100k) |
  | Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | 0.54 |
  | gpt-5.4-nano | 0.34 |
  | DeepSeek V4.1 Flash | 0.26 peak / 0.13 off-peak |

  This compares prices only. Different models need different numbers of tokens and turns for the same job (Cursor saw workers under a Fable planner use several times as many tokens), so real cost per task will not scale exactly with this table.
- **Haiku 5.5's 100k-token threshold matters for agents:** an agent whose context grows past 100,000 tokens pays 5x on every request. A cheap-worker design therefore needs short-lived, narrowly scoped workers; otherwise the Haiku rate is $0.87/h, not $0.17/h.
- **Routed fleet estimate:** 10% of active hours on Opus 5.5 (planning, judging) and 90% on Haiku 5.5 gives $0.68/h blended (workers under 100k) to $1.31/h (workers over 100k), i.e. 13–25% of the all-Opus cost. Cursor's measured hybrid landed at 12.7% of the solo-frontier run ($1,339 / $10,565), which is consistent.
- **GDPR caveat on the cheapest options:** DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM and Qwen first-party APIs process data in China; for anything containing personal or client data this adds a third-country transfer problem that the price table does not show (see section 5). Open-weight models hosted on EU infrastructure avoid this but I found no priced, sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No sourced numbers for self-hosted inference (GPU rental cost per agent-hour, throughput) were gathered; this option is unquantified here.
- Kimi, GLM and Qwen prices were not verified on official pages.

## 4. Break-even: worked examples for 100 agents

### Takeaway
At 100 agents, 8 hours a day, 20% duty cycle (160 active agent-hours per day), model cost is about **$18k/month on Opus 5.5 via API, $2.4k–6k/month with a frontier planner plus Haiku-class workers, and about $1.4k–1.8k/month on Ultra subscriptions if the early-2026 cap measurement still holds**. Break-even revenue is therefore about $5.50, $0.75–1.80 and $0.40 per active agent-hour respectively, before the operator's own time, which is the real constraint because every sellable output still needs human review.

### Inferences
All figures below are my arithmetic from the cited inputs. Fleet definition: 100 agents x 8 h/day x 20% duty = 160 active agent-hours/day; 22 working days = 3,520 active agent-hours/month (800 scheduled agent-hours/day, 17,600/month). "Active agent-hour" = one hour of model API time at Profile A.

- **Example 1: everything on Opus 5.5 via API.**
  - Model: 160 h x $5.20 = **$832/day**, **$18,304/month** (30-day operation: $24,960). On Sonnet 5.5: $416/day, $9,152/month. On Fable 5.2: $1,379/day, $30,342/month.
  - Infrastructure: Managed Agents runtime $0.08 x 160 = $12.80/day ($282/month) if sessions run only while active, or $0.08 x 800 = $64/day ($1,408/month) if all 100 sessions stay in `running` for the whole 8 hours. Self-hosted alternative: ten CX43-class VMs about EUR 160/month if available.
  - Break-even revenue: about $18,600–19,700/month, i.e. **$5.28–5.60 per active agent-hour**, $1.06–1.12 per scheduled agent-hour, or about $5–10 per ticket-sized task before review time.
  - Data residency variant: via Bedrock/Vertex EU regional endpoints add 10% (about $20,100/month model cost); Batch API (where latency allows, not for Managed Agents) would halve input and output prices.
- **Example 2: frontier planner/judge (10% of active hours on Opus 5.5) + Haiku 5.5 workers (90%).**
  - Model: $0.68/h blended, **$108/day, $2,383/month** if worker prompts stay under 100k tokens; $1.31/h, $209/day, $4,593/month if they exceed it. With an assumed 1.5x rework factor on worker hours: $2,660–5,974/month.
  - Plus the same infrastructure. Break-even revenue: about **$0.75–1.80 per active agent-hour**.
- **Example 3: Ultra subscriptions ($200 each).**
  - 7 plans (22 days) to 9 plans (30 days): **$1,400–1,800/month**, about **$0.40 per active agent-hour**, assuming the Mar 2026 measured cap ($1,100 API-equivalent per week per plan at Opus 4.6 prices) still applies.
  - Not comparable on risk: caps are opaque and discretionary, third-party harnesses are excluded, headless billing has been in flux, there is no DPA, and Fable is capped at half the weekly limit. Suitable at most for the operator's own non-client workloads.
- **Sensitivity to duty cycle:** cost scales linearly. 50% duty triples Example 1 to about $45,760/month; 100 agents concurrently active 24/7 is about $374k/month on Opus 5.5.
- **German tax interaction:** break-even revenue in Example 1 (about EUR 200k+/year) is far above the Kleinunternehmer ceiling (EUR 100,000 in the current year), so a fleet of this size cannot be run as Kleinunternehmer; below the ceiling, a Kleinunternehmer pays 19% reverse-charge VAT on US API invoices without input-tax deduction, which raises effective model cost by 19% (sources in section 7).
- **The hidden cost line is review:** Stripe's 1,300 PRs/week are all human-reviewed. A solo operator reviewing output at, say, 10 minutes per task can clear about 48 tasks per 8-hour day; 160 active agent-hours per day will produce more than that. Throughput that cannot be reviewed has cost but no revenue. The 10-minute figure is my assumption for illustration.

## 5. German and EU law for high-volume agent businesses

### Takeaway
Germany is an opt-in jurisdiction: cold email, and any call placed by an automatic calling machine, requires prior express consent even B2B (UWG § 7), business contact data is personal data under the GDPR (CNIL fined a LinkedIn enrichment tool EUR 240,000), and first-party Anthropic API offers no EU-only processing, so client work with personal or secrecy-protected data has to run through Bedrock/Vertex EU regions or equivalent with a DPA and, for tax advisers and lawyers, an additional secrecy undertaking. Since 2 August 2026 the AI Act's transparency duties for chatbots and deepfakes apply, and German courts already attribute chatbot statements to the operator.

### Cited Findings
**Outbound: cold email and cold calling**
- **Statute text [P]:** § 7 Abs. 2 UWG: an unreasonable nuisance is always to be assumed (Nr. 1) "bei Werbung mit einem Telefonanruf gegenüber einem Verbraucher ohne dessen vorherige ausdrückliche Einwilligung oder gegenüber einem sonstigen Marktteilnehmer ohne dessen zumindest mutmaßliche Einwilligung", and (Nr. 2) "bei Werbung unter Verwendung einer automatischen Anrufmaschine, eines Faxgerätes oder elektronischer Post, ohne dass eine vorherige ausdrückliche Einwilligung des Adressaten vorliegt". Nr. 3 additionally prohibits messages that conceal the sender's identity or lack a valid opt-out address. — [gesetze-im-internet.de, § 7 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)
- **Only exception for email [P]:** § 7 Abs. 3 UWG, four cumulative conditions: address obtained from the customer in connection with a sale, used for own similar goods or services, no objection, and a clear opt-out notice at collection and in every message. — [gesetze-im-internet.de, § 7 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)
- **Chamber of commerce reading [SR]:** IHK München treats genuine cold calling as impermissible towards consumers and in B2B; other commentators stress that presumed consent suffices for B2B calls but is interpreted narrowly by courts. — [IHK München](https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/ratgeber/recht/werbung-fairer-wettbewerb/marketing-per-email-telefon-brief-etc/); [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/Kommentar/11758/Telefonkontakt_B2B.php); [WBS Legal](https://www.wbs.legal/it-und-internet-recht/datenschutzrecht/achtung-beim-direktmarketing-das-muessen-sie-bei-kaltakquise-beachten-78731/)

### Inferences
- **AI voice agents for cold calls:** § 7 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UWG requires prior express consent for advertising "unter Verwendung einer automatischen Anrufmaschine" with no B2B relaxation. An AI voice bot placing outbound sales calls plausibly falls under this, which would remove even the narrow "presumed consent" route that exists for human B2B calls. I found no court decision or Bundesnetzagentur statement on this point; a lawyer should confirm before building anything on outbound voice agents.
- **Cold email at volume is structurally closed in Germany.** No B2B exception exists; each unsolicited message is a separate violation that a competitor or association can pursue by Abmahnung. An agent fleet multiplies exposure rather than revenue. Direct messages on LinkedIn or other platforms are likely "elektronische Post" as well; I did not source this and it should be verified.
- **Where US advice does not transfer:** (1) CAN-SPAM's opt-out model and "B2B cold email is legal" claims do not apply; Germany requires opt-in. (2) "Business emails are not personal data" is wrong under the GDPR, as the KASPR decision shows. (3) US fair-use arguments for scraping do not apply; the EU has a TDM exception with a rights-holder opt-out, and personal data is governed separately by the GDPR. (4) "The AI did it" is not a defence; OLG Hamm attributes chatbot output to the operator. (5) US advice on LLCs and sales tax has no bearing on German VAT, reverse charge and trade registration.
- **Client-data architecture that follows from the sources:** Anthropic first-party API under Commercial Terms plus DPA and SCCs is a lawful transfer route in principle but offers no EU-only processing or storage; for clients who demand EU residency, or for § 203 data, Bedrock or Vertex in EU regions (plus 10%) is the documented path, at the price of losing Batch, Managed Agents and server-side tools on Bedrock. Consumer subscriptions (Pro/Max/Ultra) are not covered by the DPA page and should not process client personal data. For tax advisers and lawyers the secrecy undertaking under § 62a StBerG (lawyers have an analogous provision in the BRAO, not sourced here) is the client's obligation, but as a subcontractor the operator will be asked to sign it and to flow it down to the cloud provider, which is the hard part with US hyperscalers.
- **AI Act role of a solo operator:** typically deployer, sometimes provider of a chatbot or content tool. Practical duties since 2 Aug 2026: tell people they are talking to an AI, label deepfakes, and label AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest unless it has undergone human review with editorial responsibility. The exact wording of Article 50 was not opened in this research and should be checked on EUR-Lex.
- **Chinese model APIs** (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, Qwen) have no adequacy decision behind them; using them for personal or client data needs separate legal assessment, which largely confines them to non-personal workloads.
- **Retention on the top model:** Anthropic developer reference material bundled with Claude Code (cached 3 Nov 2026, no public URL opened) states that Claude Fable 5.2 requires 30-day data retention and is not available under zero data retention unless expressly authorized. If a client contract demands zero retention, that rules out Fable 5.2 on the first-party API; verify on Anthropic's API and data retention page.

- One legislative tracker says the Article 50(2) grace period is six months rather than ending 2 Dec 2026; the timeline site and several law firms say 2 Dec 2026. Check Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in the Official Journal. White & Case's alert returned HTTP 403 and was used only via search summary.

## 6. Platform rules that kill volume plays, and documented enforcement in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Every major distribution platform now enforces against scale itself rather than against AI as such: Google's scaled-content-abuse policy with manual-action waves since mid-2025 and spam updates through September 2026, YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy with a January 2026 purge of 16 channels holding 35 million subscribers, Apple's June 2026 tightening against low-effort apps, Gmail's 0.3% spam-rate ceiling, and LinkedIn's blanket ban on automation. The model provider is a platform too: Anthropic enforced against third-party and automated subscription use three times in early 2026.

### Inferences
- The common enforcement trigger is the **pattern** (templated, high-volume, low differentiation, no accountable human), not the tool. A fleet's native output pattern is exactly what these policies describe. Volume plays in SEO content, faceless video, template apps and KDP books are the business models most directly targeted in 2025–2026.
- Platform risk compounds with German law: a banned LinkedIn or sender domain is a business interruption; an Abmahnung or GDPR complaint for the same campaign is a separate legal cost.
- Cold email economics fail twice in Germany: legally (UWG opt-in) and technically (a 0.3% complaint ceiling is three complaints per 1,000 delivered messages).
- Models that survive these rules use agents for depth per customer rather than breadth per platform: client-commissioned work, internal automation, research and analysis delivered under the operator's name with human review.

## 7. German business formalities when agents act autonomously

### Takeaway
The operator is the contracting and liable party for everything the agents say and deliver; German courts already treat a chatbot's statements as the company's own. On the tax side, the Kleinunternehmer thresholds are EUR 25,000 (prior year) and EUR 100,000 (current year), reverse-charge VAT on US API invoices applies even to Kleinunternehmer without input-tax deduction, and E-Rechnung issuing becomes mandatory on 1 Jan 2027 only above EUR 800,000 prior-year revenue and for all B2B from 1 Jan 2028, with Kleinunternehmer exempt from issuing.

### Cited Findings
- **Kleinunternehmer thresholds [SR, consistent across guides]:** since 1 Jan 2025 (Jahressteuergesetz 2024) and still valid in 2026: prior-year revenue up to EUR 25,000 and current-year revenue up to EUR 100,000; crossing EUR 100,000 during the year triggers regular taxation immediately from that transaction. One calculator site lists different figures (22,500 / 50,000) and appears outdated. — [IHK Berlin](https://www.ihk.de/berlin/service-und-beratung/recht-und-steuern/steuern-und-finanzen/kleinunternehmerregelung-4408200); [Lexware](https://www.lexware.de/wissen/buchhaltung-finanzen/kleinunternehmerregelung/)
- **Reverse charge on foreign services [SR]:** § 13b UStG makes no distinction between regular businesses and Kleinunternehmer: a Kleinunternehmer receiving services from a foreign business must declare and pay the VAT but cannot deduct it as input tax, so it is a real cost; foreign providers sometimes treat customers without a VAT ID as consumers and charge VAT themselves. — [IHK Ostwestfalen: Steuerschuldnerschaft](https://www.ihk.de/ostwestfalen/recht-und-steuern/steuern/steuerschuldnerschaft-6720846); [Accountable help: § 13b filing as Kleinunternehmer](https://help.accountable.eu/en/articles/15947088-filing-a-vat-pre-submission-on-elster-as-a-kleinunternehmer-how-it-works-for-transactions-under-13b-ustg); [Deubner: Reverse Charge bei ausländischem Leistenden](https://www.deubner-steuern.de/themen/umsatzsteuer/wechsel-der-steuerschuldnerschaft/reverse-charge-wenn-der-leistende-unternehmer-im-ausland-sitzt.html)
- **E-Rechnung timeline [SR, consistent]:** since 1 Jan 2025 every business must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 Jan 2027 businesses with more than EUR 800,000 prior-year revenue must issue them for domestic B2B; from 1 Jan 2028 all B2B businesses; until then paper/PDF remains allowed for smaller businesses; Kleinunternehmer are exempt from issuing but must be able to receive; per ETL (as of 19 Oct 2026) the latest BMF letter does not extend the transition periods, although trade bodies have asked for a delay. — [ETL: Zeitplan](https://www.etl.de/e-rechnung/zeitplan/); [Haufe: E-Rechnung](https://www.haufe.de/wissen/finance/e-rechnung); [ZDH](https://www.zdh.de/ueber-uns/fachbereich-steuern-und-finanzen/elektronische-rechnung/); [Agicap](https://agicap.com/de/artikel/e-rechnung-fristen/)
- **Who is responsible for agent output [S]:** OLG Hamm (4 UKl 3/25): the chatbot is not a third party; statements made within the company's business organization are the company's own; autonomy of the AI is no defence (not final). — [LTO, 12 May 2026](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/olg-hamm-aerzte-haften-chatbot-falsche-facharztbezeichnung-ki-uwg)
- **Software as a product from Dec 2026 [SR]:** the new product liability regime covers software and AI systems placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026. — [IHK Frankfurt](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/recht/uebersicht-alle-rechtsthemen/vertragsrecht-und-e-commerce-recht/uebersicht/neue-regelungen-zur-produkthaftung-2026-7177184)
- **Insurance [SR]:** AI-assisted work is covered by an IT-Haftpflicht / Vermögensschadenhaftpflicht only if the tariff does not exclude it and AI use fits the insured activity description, described by one broker as the central tariff question in 2026; typical sums insured for IT service providers EUR 300,000–500,000, EUR 1 million for higher risk; older policies often predate AI-specific risks; Hiscox "Net IT" brochures (Sep 2025) mention neither an AI exclusion nor premiums. — [versicherungsmakler.ac](https://versicherungsmakler.ac/it-haftpflichtversicherung/); [compound.law](https://compound.law/de-DE/compliance/haftpflichtversicherung-ki-entwickler/); [Hiscox Net IT highlight sheet 09/2025](https://brochureware.hiscox.de/sites/default/files/documents/highlightsheet-net-it-by-hiscox-092025.pdf)
- **Anthropic invoices from the US [P]:** "All prices are in USD" on the API pricing page (VAT treatment is not stated there); usage credits on subscriptions are billed at standard API rates. — [Claude API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-using-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)

### Inferences
- **Contracting party:** contracts, offers and emails sent by agents are the operator's; by extension of the OLG Hamm reasoning the operator cannot point to the model. Practical consequence: no agent should send binding offers, legal or tax statements, or client deliverables without a human release step, and client contracts should describe AI use, review responsibility and liability caps. Contract-law attribution itself is unsourced here (see Gaps).
- **Kleinunternehmer and fleets do not mix beyond a pilot:** at Example 1 scale the required revenue exceeds EUR 100,000 several times over. Below the ceiling, the non-deductible 19% reverse charge makes API spend 19% more expensive (for example $2,383/month becomes about $2,836). B2B clients (tax firms) can deduct VAT anyway, so opting for regular taxation is usually the better fit for an API-heavy B2B service; confirm with a tax adviser.
- **E-Rechnung:** a solo operator below EUR 800,000 has until 31 Dec 2027 before issuing becomes mandatory, but must already be able to receive e-invoices; agent-generated invoices must still meet § 14 UStG content rules.
- **Insurance:** ask the insurer in writing whether (a) autonomous agent output delivered to clients, (b) data protection breaches by sub-processors, and (c) unfair-competition claims from agent communications are covered; AI clauses differ by tariff and US-style explicit AI exclusions were reported entering the market in 2026 (US source, not directly transferable).

### Gaps
- No primary text of § 19 UStG, § 13b UStG or the BMF letters on E-Rechnung was opened; all tax statements come from chamber and vendor guides and need a tax adviser's confirmation.
- No German case law or authoritative commentary was retrieved on contract formation by AI agents or on the allocation of liability between operator and client for agent-produced work (Werkvertrag vs Dienstvertrag, acceptance, warranty).
- No premium figures and no confirmed AI clauses for specific German tariffs (Hiscox, exali, Markel) were found.
- Trade registration questions (Gewerbe vs freiberuflich for AI services, Gewerbesteuer) and Impressum / information duties for agent-run websites were not researched.
- Whether Anthropic charges German VAT on consumer subscriptions versus reverse charge on API invoices with a VAT ID was not verified.
